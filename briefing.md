# JWST Research Briefing

Journal-club research briefing (sources: Wikipedia's James Webb Space Telescope and Hubble Space Telescope articles)

## Overview

JWST is a space telescope designed to conduct infrared astronomy - the largest telescope in space. Its gold-coated beryllium primary mirror (18 hexagonal segments) is 6.5 m (21 ft) across, with a total collecting area of about 25.4 m2 (273 ft2), roughly six times Hubble's. Launched 25 December 2021 on an Ariane 5, Webb arrived at the Sun-Earth L2 point - about 1.5 million km (930,000 mi) from Earth - in January 2022, and its first image went public on 11 July 2022. NASA led design and development with the European Space Agency and the Canadian Space Agency; Northrop Grumman was the primary contractor. Webb's mass is about half Hubble's; its instruments must observe below 50 K (-223 C), protected by a five-layer sunshield (deployed 46.46 x 69.54 ft).

## Science goals and instruments (per the article's "Features" section)

Webb observes in the near infrared, with its instruments covering orange/red visible light and mid-infrared (0.6-28.5 microns). The article gives four reasons for prioritizing infrared: high-redshift light is redshifted into the infrared and is otherwise unobservable; infrared passes more easily through dust clouds than visible light; colder objects such as debris disks and planets emit most strongly in the infrared; and these infrared bands are hard to study from the ground or with earlier space telescopes. Webb can detect objects up to 100 times fainter than Hubble can, and objects as early as redshift z~20 (about 180 million years after the Big Bang); Hubble cannot see further back than very early reionization at z~11.1. The Integrated Science Instrument Module (ISIM) provides electrical power, computing, cooling, and structural stability, and holds four science instruments plus a guide camera: NIRCam, NIRSpec, MIRI, and NIRISS.

## Hubble Space Telescope (per the Hubble article)

Hubble's 2.4 m (7 ft 10 in) mirror observes ultraviolet, visible, and near-infrared light from low Earth orbit (550 km / 340 mi altitude). It is the only telescope designed to be serviced in space by astronauts: five Space Shuttle missions repaired, upgraded, and replaced systems, including all five of the main instruments; the first (STS-61, 1993) corrected a defective primary mirror. Hubble completed 30 years of operation in April 2020 and is predicted to last until 2030-2040. Hubble is the visible-light predecessor to JWST.
